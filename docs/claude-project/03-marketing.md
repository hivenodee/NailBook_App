# PoroBook — Marketing & Positioning

Use this when asking Claude about copy, audience targeting, growth strategy, competitor positioning, or launch planning.

---

## One-sentence positioning

> **PoroBook is the editorial-grade booking platform built specifically for Black beauty professionals.**

Long form:
> PoroBook is where independent Black beauty pros — nail techs, hair stylists, lash and brow artists, estheticians — take bookings, hold deposits, and present their work. The booking page feels like a magazine spread. The whole platform is designed for the audience that's currently DMing through bookings on Instagram.

## The "why now / why us" story

Three honest tailwinds:
1. **Black beauty pros are massively underserved by existing tools.** Square, Acuity, Vagaro, GlossGenius, StyleSeat — all built for chains or generic small-business. None have brand register, photography style, or copy treatment that respects the audience.
2. **Instagram DM booking is the actual incumbent.** It's broken (missed messages, no deposits, no calendar sync), but it's free and familiar. The displacement opportunity is huge but only if the alternative is good enough to be worth switching.
3. **Editorial luxury aesthetic is undervalued in beauty SaaS.** Aesop, Pat McGrath Labs, Glossier set the visual register; SaaS for the same audience looks like 2012 Bootstrap. PoroBook closes that gap.

## Target audience

### Primary — beauty providers (the customer paying for the platform eventually)

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Identity | Black, primarily women, 25–45 |
| Service type | Nail techs first (founder-market fit), then hair, lash, brow, esthetician, massage |
| Setup | Independent — home studio, suite rental, or single-chair shop |
| Volume | 5–25 appointments per week (~$2k–$15k monthly revenue) |
| Current tools | Instagram DMs (most common), Square, GlossGenius, Vagaro, Acuity |
| Platforms they live on | Instagram (everyone), TikTok (under 35), sometimes Threads |
| Pain points | Missed DM bookings, no-shows without deposit protection, manually sending reminders, no centralized client history, generic SaaS aesthetic doesn't reflect their work |

### Secondary — beauty clients (the users who book)

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| Identity | Mostly Black women, 18–45, urban + suburban |
| Booking trigger | Comes from a provider's Instagram bio link or shared screenshot |
| Device | Phone, 95%+ |
| Account willingness | Will NOT create an account just to book. Will pay a deposit if it's clearly disclosed. |
| Behavior | Books, attends, leaves, rebooks same provider repeatedly. Doesn't shop across the platform like Uber Eats. |

The platform optimizes for the **client→provider** relationship being repeat-business, not marketplace-discovery. Discovery (`/explore`) exists but is secondary.

## Competitor landscape

| Competitor | What they do well | What they miss |
|---|---|---|
| **Square Appointments** | Free, deep POS integration, mature scheduling | Generic UI, designed for retail-shop chains, no brand register for individual artists |
| **GlossGenius** | Beauty-focused, decent design, instant booking, marketing tools | $39+/mo subscription, still feels SaaS-y, not editorial |
| **Vagaro** | Comprehensive features, big provider directory | Cluttered UI, dated visual language, generic stock photography |
| **Acuity (by Squarespace)** | Powerful customization, polished | Calendar-first not service-first, no built-in deposit/payment flow on free tier |
| **StyleSeat** | Marketplace + booking combo, mobile-first | Take-rate model, low-quality listings, race-to-the-bottom pricing pressure |
| **Booksy** | Strong in Eastern Europe + US growth, free for solo | UI feels Uber-driver-style, not luxury |
| **Calendly / Cal.com** | Beautifully designed booking flows | Generic — no portfolio, no service catalog, no deposit/payment, no industry context |

### Where PoroBook wins
- **Editorial visual register** that matches the work providers actually do (luxury salon, not retail SaaS)
- **Specifically for Black beauty pros** — every marketing surface and product decision filters through that
- **No-account booking** — clients book in 60 seconds via shareable link without friction
- **Deposit + balance flow built in** — not bolted on, not a paid upgrade
- **Mobile-first public flow** — the booking link works perfectly on a phone, which is where 95% of clients come from

### Where PoroBook does NOT compete (and shouldn't try to)
- POS hardware (Square's moat)
- Multi-staff scheduling for salon chains (Vagaro / Mindbody territory)
- Education / classes marketplace (deferred)
- Cross-vertical scheduling (lawyers, therapists, etc.)

## Brand voice + register

The brand register is **editorial luxury** — Aesop, Pat McGrath Labs, Mytopicals, Resy. Specifically:

### What we sound like
- **Confident, never apologetic.** "Books open Thursday at noon," not "We're so excited to share that our books are opening!"
- **Short sentences. Specific nouns and verbs.** "Booked. Take a moment." not "You've successfully completed your booking transaction."
- **Sentence case for UI labels** — "Book now," not "BOOK NOW" or "Book Now" inconsistent.
- **No corporate filler** — no "leverage," "seamless," "empower," "unlock potential," "robust solution."
- **No exclamation points** unless celebrating something specific.
- **No em-dashes in production copy** (style choice).

### What we don't sound like
- Not "girl boss" voice ("YASS babe go off!")
- Not utility SaaS ("Streamline your booking workflow")
- Not luxury parody ("Bespoke. Curated. Elevated.")
- Not sales-y ("Limited time! Only 3 spots left!")
- Not corporate jargon ("synergies," "stakeholders," "scale")

### Tone markers (test copy against these)
- A high-end nail artist would feel comfortable sending the link to her clientele
- A first-time client would book without thinking "this feels sketchy"
- A reader of *Cherry Bombe*, *The Cut*, or *NYT Wirecutter* would find it appropriate

## Visual identity (summary — see `05-design-system.md` for full)

- **Palette**: cream (#FBF8F3) + rust (#C4732A) + ink (#1A1614). No neons, no gradients.
- **Typography**: Playfair Display (serif, headings) + DM Sans / Inter (sans, body).
- **Photography**: editorial style, warm natural light, real Black women, no stock-default casting.
- **Layout**: lots of whitespace, asymmetric editorial composition, sharp rectangles, minimal shadows.

## Channels (for launch and beyond)

In priority order:

### Tier 1 — Direct + organic (Months 1–3)

| Channel | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Founder's personal Instagram + TikTok** | Behind-the-scenes building content, founder story, beta cohort spotlights |
| **Beta cohort's existing audiences** | Each beta provider shares their PoroBook link in their bio. ~10k–50k impressions per provider per month, free. |
| **WhatsApp / SMS referral chains** | Black beauty pro community is tight. One satisfied user tells 3. |
| **Reddit / Discord / FB groups** | r/BlackHair, r/NailArt, Black-woman-owned business Discords, local FB pro groups. Genuine participation, not spammy posting. |

### Tier 2 — Earned + community (Months 3–6)

| Channel | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Press placements** | Black-founder press: Blavity, Essence, Refinery29 Unbothered. Tech press: Tech.co Black Founders, Fortune Inc, AfroTech |
| **Founder interviews / podcasts** | Side Hustle Pro, Brown Ambition, Therapy for Black Girls (if relevant), beauty-industry podcasts |
| **Community events** | Sponsor or attend Black beauty pro meetups in 3 target cities (Atlanta, NYC, LA, Houston) |

### Tier 3 — Paid + scaled (Month 6+, only if Tier 1/2 produce traction)

| Channel | Approach |
|---|---|
| **Meta ads** (IG + FB) | Lookalike audiences from beta provider list. Test $500/mo for 3 months, measure CAC vs LTV before scaling. |
| **TikTok ads** | Native-feeling video creative, NOT product demos. Behind-the-counter, "how I run my nail business" content. |
| **Influencer partnerships** | Mid-tier Black beauty influencers (50k–500k). Free use of platform + revenue share. |

### What NOT to do
- **No paid ads before Tier 1/2 produce signal.** Don't pay to acquire users you can't keep.
- **No Twitter / X for marketing.** Audience isn't there in the right numbers.
- **No Google Search ads.** Search intent for booking is mostly captured by Square / Acuity SEO; too expensive to compete.
- **No Black History Month-only campaigns.** The brand is year-round, not seasonal.

## Beta plan

| Phase | Goal | Cohort size | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Closed alpha** | Validate the booking flow with people who'll tell you the truth | 2–3 friends or close acquaintances | Now → Phase D deploy |
| **Friends-and-family beta** | Find real bugs, refine onboarding, prove the loop closes | 3–5 hand-picked providers via personal network | Days 8–30 after Phase D |
| **Invite-only beta** | Grow the cohort, validate referral mechanics | 15–25 providers, each referred by an existing beta provider | Months 2–3 |
| **Public beta** | Open self-serve signup, with manual verification queue | 25–100 providers | Month 4+ |
| **General availability** | Remove "beta" tag, official launch | 100+ | Month 6+ |

### Beta provider criteria
- Black, primarily women, beauty pro
- Independent (not salon-employed)
- Currently using IG DM, Square, GlossGenius, Vagaro, or Acuity
- Located in founder's city (easier to onboard / fix issues quickly)
- 5–20 appointments per week (volume to surface bugs, not so much that bugs cost them money)
- Has tolerance for first-week glitches

### Onboarding SOP (per beta provider)
1. 30-minute Zoom or in-person walkthrough
2. Live signup → finish profile (cover, bio, services, hours) together
3. Manually flip `isVerified=true` in Prisma Studio during the call
4. Help them set up their first portfolio photos
5. Share the booking URL with their existing client base via Story/Highlight
6. Promise: respond to any issue within 4 business hours for the first month
7. Friday check-in DM every week for the first month

## Pricing strategy (open question — see `06-decisions-and-open-questions.md`)

Honest current state: **no decision**. Default plan is **free for the first 6 months of beta**, then evaluate.

Three viable models when paid:

| Model | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat monthly subscription** ($29–49/mo) | Predictable revenue, simple to communicate | High barrier for new providers; if they have a slow month they churn |
| **Take rate** (1.5–3% on top of Stripe fees) | Aligns with provider success; no out-of-pocket cost | Stripe will flag you as a money transmitter at scale; complex Stripe Connect setup |
| **Hybrid** ($0–9/mo with a small take rate) | Best of both | Most complex to explain |

Recommendation when ready to monetize: **flat $29/mo for the first 12 months** to keep the model simple, then add tiers later. Beauty-pro audience knows GlossGenius is $39+/mo — undercut by 25% and the value-prop is clear.

## Launch announcement template (when ready)

Single Instagram + TikTok post, drafted to:
1. Open with the problem ("Booking via DM is broken: 3am 'can I get a fill?' messages, half of them lost, no deposit safety net.")
2. Show the visual register (one editorial photo from the brand library — `editorial-hands-marble.jpeg` is the strongest)
3. Drop the link (`porobook.com`)
4. Tease invite-only beta ("First 25 providers get free use through end of year")

Don't post on Mondays or weekends (Black creator audiences peak Tuesday–Thursday 6–10pm ET).

## Metrics to watch

In priority order:

| Metric | Target (post-beta launch) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Active providers** (≥1 booking last 14 days) | Growing 10–20% week-over-week | Engagement, not signup vanity |
| **Completed bookings per provider per week** | ≥5 average | Loop is closing; not just decoration |
| **Booking flow conversion** (start → confirmed) | ≥40% | Friction signal |
| **Client return rate** (rebook same provider within 8 weeks) | ≥30% | Real product-market fit |
| **NPS from providers** | ≥40 | Will they refer others? |
| **Deposit dispute rate** | <1% of bookings | Money flow is clean |

Don't chase signups or page views. Chase the loop closing — booking → payment → completion → rebooking.
